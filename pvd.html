Pressure

Argon is what the plasma is made of, but it also sits in the way. The mean free path is how far a sputtered atom flies before hitting an argon atom. At low pressure it is several centimeters, so atoms arrive straight from the target with energy to spare. At high pressure they bounce around, slow down, and arrive from random directions, which makes porous films and wipes out GLAD shadowing.

Temperature

What matters is the substrate temperature as a fraction of the metal’s melting point. Copper at room temperature is already at 0.22, so its atoms can shuffle into place. Tungsten at room temperature is at 0.08, so they stick where they land. That is why the same settings give a dense copper film and a porous tungsten one.

Angle and rotation

Tilting the substrate far over makes every bump shadow the area behind it, and the film grows as tilted columns. Rotating the substrate during growth averages the direction out, so the columns straighten into posts. This only works when atoms arrive in straight lines, meaning low pressure.

Model notes: mean free path from kinetic theory (λ = kT/√2πd²p, d ≈ 0.35 nm). An atom counts as arriving in a straight line if it has fewer than 4 collisions on the way. Sputter yields at 600 eV Ar⁺ from Angstrom Sciences. Structure zones after Thornton (1974), with approximate boundaries.
