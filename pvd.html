The simulator

Sample: MgO(001)

A small MgO crystal: a square grid of atoms. Molybdenum fits on it turned 45° (5.7% off), so a hot film can copy it: epitaxy.

Steps follow a published procedure from the Gall group at RPI: epitaxial Mo(001) and Mo(011) layers. Films in the study ran from about 4 to 400 nm thick.

Why heat the MgO to 900 °C?

Same Mo run, hot vs. room temperature. Hot grows as one teal crystal; cold breaks into many small grains.

Does the sample need to be a crystal?

Hot Mo on MgO, then on glass, which has no pattern for the film to copy.

Why use so little gas?

Copper with a little argon, then a lot. Watch the hit colors and the gaps.

Why put titanium down first?

Copper alone, then on a thin Ti layer, which helps it stick (the chemistry isn’t simulated).

Sputtering

Argon ions hit a metal target and knock atoms off. They fly to the sample and stack up into a coating.

Gas

More argon means more hits on the way. Each hit slows and turns an atom; after a few it just drifts.

Heat

A hot sample lets landed atoms slide into gaps. “Hot” is relative to the metal’s melting point.

Angle

Tilt the sample and atoms shadow each other into slanted columns. Spinning straightens them.

Model notes: mean free path from kinetic theory; hits are elastic collisions with argon; atoms launch at 5 eV; slowed atoms drift 5× faster than real so you can see them land; zones after Thornton (1974). Numbers are trends, not predictions.

Sources

  1. Example run and Mo-on-MgO alignment: Gall group, “Anisotropic Resistivity Size Effect in Epitaxial Mo(001) and Mo(011) Layers” (open access, PubMed Central).
  2. Structure zones: J. A. Thornton, J. Vac. Sci. Technol. 11, 666 (1974). Zone boundaries here are approximate.
  3. Sputter yields at 600 eV Ar⁺: Angstrom Sciences sputter yield table.
  4. Distance between gas hits: hard-sphere kinetic theory, λ = kT / (√2·π·d²·p), with an assumed d ≈ 0.35 nm.
  5. Lattice constants (Cu, Al, Mo, W, Ti, Ru, MgO) and melting points: standard room-temperature handbook values.
  6. Gun voltage (≈350 V), coating speeds, and the growth rules are my own simplified estimates, not measurements from any system.
