Why the cut matters

A film grown on a crystal tends to line its atoms up with the surface it lands on. That is templating, or epitaxy. C-plane and R-plane sapphire are the same crystal cut two ways, so they hand a film two different atomic patterns to copy.

Amorphous vs. crystalline

An amorphous substrate (like glass or thermal oxide) has no long-range order, so there is no pattern to copy and films come out amorphous or randomly oriented. Annealing can crystallize a film afterward, but only a crystalline substrate can set its orientation.

Reading the numbers

d is the spacing between neighboring parallel planes, which is what X-ray diffraction measures. Hexagonal crystals use four indices (h k i l) with i = −(h + k), so you type three and the fourth fills itself in.
