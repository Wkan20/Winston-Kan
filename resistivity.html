    Bulk metal

    Electrons in a metal travel an average distance λ, the mean free path, before bumping into a vibrating atom. Those collisions set the bulk resistivity ρ₀. Copper has the lowest ρ₀ of these metals but the longest λ, about 40 nm.

    Surfaces

    Once a film is not much thicker than λ, electrons keep hitting its top and bottom surfaces. Rough surfaces scatter them randomly (p = 0) and add resistance; perfectly smooth ones reflect them like a mirror (p = 1) and add none. This is the Fuchs–Sondheimer model.

    Grain boundaries

    Sputtered films are made of many small crystals. Each boundary between them reflects some electrons (R), and thinner films have smaller grains and more boundaries. This is the Mayadas–Shatzkes model. The tool adds both effects to ρ₀, a common simplification.

    Bulk ρ₀ and λ: D. Gall, “Electron mean free path in elemental metals,” J. Appl. Phys. 119, 085101 (2016). Co and Ru are hexagonal; λ is the average of the two crystal directions.
