Featured project

Substrate Plane Viewer

Every chip starts on a wafer cut along a specific crystal plane: silicon (100) for most CMOS, C-plane sapphire for GaN LEDs. I could recite those Miller indices but couldn't picture the cuts inside the crystal, so I built a tool that draws them. It takes any plane in a cubic or hexagonal crystal, slices it through the unit cell in 3D, and reports the plane spacing and the angle to a second plane.

The math

Each plane is the set of points where the reciprocal-lattice vector G satisfies G·r = n. Its length gives d = 1/|G|.

The check

Results match reference values: R-plane sapphire d = 3.48 Å, 57.6° from C-plane; Si (111) d = 3.136 Å.

The use

Comparing wafer and substrate orientations, and matching XRD peaks to the planes that produce them.

Project

GLAD Film Simulator

The lab I work in grows films by sputtering, including nanostructured films made by glancing-angle deposition. I noticed nothing modeled the growth process itself, so I built a simple atom-by-atom simulation of it. Atoms fly in at an angle, stick on contact, and hop if the substrate is hot. Tilting the flux makes tall features shadow the area behind them, and the film breaks up into columns.

The model

Three rules per atom: fly, stick, hop. Columns, posts, and zigzags emerge on their own from shadowing.

The check

Density falls from 48% at normal incidence to 6% at 85°, and columns lean toward the flux, a bit under Tait's rule as grid models usually do.

The use

Trying angles, temperatures, and rotation in seconds before spending hours of chamber time.
