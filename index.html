Substrate Plane Viewer

Every chip starts on a wafer cut along a specific crystal plane: silicon (100) for most CMOS, C-plane sapphire for GaN LEDs. I could recite the Miller indices but couldn’t picture the cuts, so I built a tool that slices any plane through a cubic or hexagonal unit cell in 3D.

Model
Planes from the reciprocal-lattice vector G; spacing d = 1/|G|.
Checked
Sapphire R-plane d = 3.48 Å and 57.6° from C-plane; Si (111) d = 3.136 Å.

GLAD Film Simulator

Sputtered atoms that arrive at a glancing angle get caught by every bump, and the film breaks up into tilted columns. This model grows a film one atom at a time with three rules: fly in at an angle, stick on contact, hop if the substrate is hot.

Model
2D atom-by-atom deposition with shadowing, surface mobility, and substrate rotation.
Checked
Density falls from 48% at normal incidence to 6% at 85°; columns lean toward the flux, a little under Tait’s rule.

Thin-Wire Resistivity

Copper is the best wiring metal in bulk, but in a film a few nanometers thick its electrons keep hitting surfaces and grain boundaries, and its resistance climbs. This explorer models that size effect for six chip-wiring metals and shows where copper stops being the best choice.

Model
Fuchs–Sondheimer surface scattering plus Mayadas–Shatzkes grain-boundary scattering.
Checked
Matches the thick-film limit 1 + 3λ(1−p)/8d to within 0.2%; fits recover known parameters.
